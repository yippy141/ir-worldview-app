## Recommended launch case

The launch recommendation is **Europe’s anti-ballistic-missile coalition around Ukraine**. It is the strongest first case because the official record now shows a concrete coalition of ten states, a linked French-Ukrainian industrial package, a NATO summit that raised the political floor for European defence spending and support to Ukraine, and simultaneous evidence that U.S. force availability to Europe is under review and already being reduced in some NATO planning pools. That makes the central analytical question unusually clean: **is Europe moving from dependency toward partial substitution, or merely adding a thin European layer on top of still-essential U.S. enablers?** citeturn27view0turn26view0turn26view1turn30view1turn30view0turn29news30

## Archive case

This archive case is still highly consequential, but it is better as an archive item than as the lead launch. The reason is not that it lacks importance; it is that the core mechanism is clearer and narrower. Washington has now shown that, after legal setbacks to broader emergency tariffs, it can use Section 301 to impose a more selective and legally sturdier form of coercive trade pressure. Brazil, meanwhile, is trying to retaliate without imposing maximum pain on itself, while also cushioning affected sectors at home. That gives the case strong value for economic-statecraft diagnosis, but less breadth than the launch case. citeturn31view0turn31view3turn38news32turn38news29turn35search1turn38search0
