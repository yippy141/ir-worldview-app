The sprint contract defers four substantive workstreams:

1. hierarchy/authority construct and item coverage;
2. threshold/irreversibility construct and item coverage;
3. Tier 2 raw-answer storage with explicit opt-in and first-party controls;

## V22 archetype-review pointers

1. **Observe before renaming.** Test whether readers can explain the name,
   posture, and gloss in their own words. Do not infer failure from internal
   taste alone.
2. **Separate the objects under review.** Name fit, analogue fit, and
   measurement fit are three different judgments. A weak analogue may require
   a different comparison, not a different score or archetype code.
3. **Review regional concepts with regional expertise.** `Shi`, `Nemawashi`,
   the ASEAN Way, and `Dependencia` need reviewers who can identify flattening,
   appropriation, or false equivalence—not merely check spelling.
4. **Keep the limitation visible.** Every analogue should retain a concise
   “where it breaks” note. This prevents an editorial comparison from becoming
   a claim that the respondent endorses a full school, person, or episode.
5. **Keep blends synthetic, not historical.** A blend should combine the two
   presentation names and a generated tension gloss, but should not invent a
   hybrid historical analogue.
6. **Evaluate the share-card line empirically.** Check whether the analogue
   helps recognition on actual platform previews or competes with the result.
   If it harms legibility, keep it on the result/evidence page rather than
   shrinking the card or overloading the footer.
7. **Localize concepts, not just strings.** Chinese work should decide how to
   explain each concept and caveat; transliteration or silent English fallback
   is not parity.
8. **Version any mapping change.** Copy-only changes remain presentation data.
   A code, lens, posture, or scoring change needs measurement evidence,
   compatibility rules, and a new version.

- Tier 1 and replay are either explicitly dormant or activated through signed
  checklists.

### Phase 1: specify hierarchy and threshold constructs

- Define each construct, rival explanations, and discriminant validity.
- Draft valence-balanced coverage with at least 40% explicit reverse coding
  per dimension.
- Show how each differs from the existing seven dimensions.
- Pre-register validation, stopping, and translation-equivalence rules.

Acceptance:

- no extreme/compromise/opposite-extreme option structure;
- item coverage exists before a family or archetype is named;
- attainable ranges and synthetic-diagnostic limits are documented.

### Phase 2: validate before adding families

- Pilot the two constructs without emitting new public identities.
- Test within-language and cross-language behavior separately.
- Establish what evidence would justify a new scoring version.

Acceptance:

- no nationality, citizenship, or culture scoring adjustment;
- no pooled-language calibration without equivalence evidence;
- no population inference from synthetic diagnostics.

### Phase 3: extend scoring by version

- Add a new scoring version without modifying V1 or V2.
- Add immutable replay fixtures and all required provenance.
- Prove every modeled family is reachable without an end-of-function override.
- Preserve legacy share identity.

Acceptance:

- strict discrimination tests run with zero skips;
- no score is printed on an unattainable scale;
- exact forms and adaptive policy versions round-trip.

### Phase 4: add hierarchy/threshold presentation mappings

- Add H/T codes only after Phase 3 acceptance.
- Complete owner naming review before public copy.
- Confirm all pure and blended combinations are reachable and legible.

### Phase 5: Tier 2 and adaptive selection

- Design Tier 2 as a separate explicit opt-in flow with retention, access,
  deletion, export, and pseudonymity language.
- Version adaptive policies and record the exact selected items.
- Keep Tier 1 aggregate-only and Tier 2 respondent-level storage logically and
  operationally separate.

## Non-goals and guardrails

- No nationality, citizenship, or culture adjustment to scoring.
- No population-percentile claim from synthetic diagnostics.
- No confidence, clarity, or settledness metric.
- No new family before item coverage.
- No hidden scorer override.
- No pooled English/Chinese calibration without equivalence evidence.
- No raw-answer collection through Tier 1.
- No automatic quiz advance.
- No modification of historical V1 or current V2 merely to simplify V22.
