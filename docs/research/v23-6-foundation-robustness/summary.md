low-differentiation records. Correlated synthetic and canonical records are

D claim, because boundary and response-style records change frequently. The
only warranted positive description is: the current resolver shows
deterministic structural stability for the tested correlated synthetic and
archetype-directed ensembles away from boundaries.

copy, or frozen fixture changed.

## Posture midpoint

The hard midpoint of 4 is reachable. It appears exactly in the core uniform,
core correlated, core boundary, full correlated, and full boundary ensembles.
Direction checks found no non-monotonic posture transitions.

Its location is form-dependent:

- Core uniform restraint has median 4; 45.00% is below, 14.67% exactly at,
  and 40.33% above.
- Full uniform restraint ranges from 4.02 to 5.15; all 160 records are above
  the hard midpoint and the median is 4.65.
- Full correlated synthetic restraint has median 4.93; 79.38% is above,
  20.00% below, and 0.63% exactly at 4.
- Targeted uniform medians range from 4.40 to 4.70 depending on pair.

The midpoint is therefore mechanically reachable but is not centered in the
extended-form synthetic distributions. This does not authorize a threshold
change. It requires an owner decision about whether a single raw midpoint is
the intended presentation rule across calibrations, especially because the
code comment currently says it remains valid for both core and extended forms.

This behavior is safe for the diagnostic but reinforces the production rule

direction violations and zero direct posture-direction violations across the

are excluded from monotonicity claims.

## Public placement-strength language

The existing bands track the same top-two gap used by blend/narrative routing,
and the observed ordering is directionally correct. In uniform core records,
full-archetype flip rates are 43.53% in `lowDifferentiation`, 24.36% in
`stableModeration`, and 3.65% in `sharplyDifferentiated`. Uniform full-form
rates are 29.24%, 14.49%, and 2.37%, respectively.

The language still overreaches at the firm end. “Firmly fixed” and “stable
enough to argue from” sound like evidence about a person's durable placement,
while the bar only describes a deterministic top-two gap under the current
model. A safer presentation would say the modeled lead is clearer, keep the
runner-up visible, and state that small-answer sensitivity rises near the
declared boundary. Core results should remain explicitly provisional.
